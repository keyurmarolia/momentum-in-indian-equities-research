# Relationship to Jegadeesh and Titman (1993)

The paper tests whether recent US stock winners continue to outperform recent losers. It buys the highest past-return decile and shorts the lowest, comparing 3-, 6-, 9- and 12-month formation and holding horizons with overlapping monthly cohorts.

## Shared question

Past return determines winner and loser ranks before holding. A winner-minus-loser reference tests relative continuation; formation and refresh choices expose sensitivity to portfolio design.

## This study

The NSE study uses 12, 24 or 50 names instead of deciles. It uses literal six- and twelve-month formation without a skipped month and one live portfolio refreshed every one, three or six months. Refresh frequency is not an overlapping-cohort holding period.

Four long-only signals compare raw momentum, volatility-adjusted momentum, Jensen alpha and KM's trend-filtered momentum. Equal-weight reset and winner drift test allocation choices. Trading charges and a simplified Indian equity-tax overlay measure friction sensitivity.

The academic reference uses 50% scheduled long and 50% short gross exposure, fixed borrowing deductions and collateral/cash earnings. It is not a direct replication of the paper's return.

## Meaning

The research asks whether momentum ranking remains useful across concentration, refresh speed, maintenance and modeled frictions in Indian equities. KM tests whether technical admission and exit-to-cash change the trade-off.

Positive returns cannot establish a behavioral cause. Data quality, missing delisting recoveries, execution assumptions, grid search and the absence of an independent holdout limit inference. Findings distinguish observed outcomes from claims the design cannot support.
